Sweet Honey Overdrive Guitar Pedal

Built a custom PCB for a guitar pedal, including editing a schematic and designing + laying out the PCB.

Process

Started by creating a simple Arduino circuit in KiCad. The MAX30102 and SSD1306 are custom-made parts.

Fast forward to first PCB completed! Got used to PCB layout, footprints, vias, DRC checks, and Gerber files.

Time for the guitar pedal now!

Reference circuit:

After spending a while learning all about op-amps, diode clipping, potentiometers, and applying all that to analyzing the circuit, I built the schematic for it in KiCad:

Wanted to add a mode clipping at a lower voltage (0.7V rather than 2.2V) to give a fuzzier sound, so added a switch here to toggle silicon and LED clipping modes.

Updated circuit diagram:

Created a list of all the parts I needed on DigiKey, and referenced them for footprints.

Okay, the board is laid out! I had to work under a pretty restrictive width of 56mm to ensure that it fits into the enclosure I plan on using. As I needed IN, OUT, and CTRL on the bottom of the pedal to connect to the footswitch, I decided to use a counter-clockwise design philosophy, starting with power coming into the right side at +9V (as the input jack for power is right hand side). This then loops around the Op-Amp and potentiometers in the center of the board, eventually reaching OUT on the other side. This helps keep the Op-Amp + Pots centered as much of the design revolves around them, and also separates the input and output channels to prevent unwanted noise. Using +VB on top layer and GND as fill on bottom layer to minimize routing. Now to wire!

Wired! Tried to avoid 90 degree angles, keep vias to a minimum and created a wider track for power (anything at 9V).

Cleaned up some silkscreen warning, and all good!

Here is the finished PCB. I would love to generate the Gerber files now, but I am going to wait until I get things confirmed on the mechanical side (pedalbox width, use of mounting holes, and if I’m going to custom make my own pedal box!) just in case the board needs tweaking.

The next step is going to be running the schematic through LTspice to make sure everything works as intended, and see if there's any optimization needed for part capacitance/resistance values. And figuring out details on the pedal box / foot switch.
